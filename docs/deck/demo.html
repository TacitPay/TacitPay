02 / 06 The problem

Paying or getting paid in crypto gives you two bad options.

Option one · transparent chain

Everyone learns what you charge, and who pays you.

Every payment permanently publishes who paid whom, and how much. Fresh addresses don't help — clustering stitches the history back together: your rate card to every competitor, your client list to anyone who looks.

Option two · fully private rails

Your only proof is the other side's paperwork.

A signed receipt can be refused, disputed, or faked by the two people who wrote it — which is why auditors ask for bank statements, not receipts. Private rails have no neutral witness at all.

Real businesses need both at once: privacy from the world, proof on demand.

03 / 06 The solution

Both at once — three promises.

Private by construction

The chain holds a commitment — never the invoice.

Amount, memo and parties stay on the parties' own devices. What lands on-chain: a hash, a status flag, an expiry.

Provable by anyone

Settlement verifies with no wallet and no permission.

One public page confirms an invoice was paid — enough for an accountant, a counterparty, or a court.

No server to trust

The invoice travels as a link — TacitPay never sees it.

The payload rides the URL fragment, which browsers never transmit. Proofs are generated where you choose — never by us.

05 / 06 Wave 2 · Sep 27 – Oct 17

The product completes, in this order.

  1. Shielded settlement on public networks. A contract-minted wrapper for browser wallets, and the Wallet SDK's native shielding once its fix ships, turn on the greyed Private card: per-invoice public or private settlement.

  2. Funds safety. Milestone escrow, claim-based refunds, and a timeout escape hatch — money can never get stuck.

  3. Zero-setup payers. Sponsored DUST: a first-time payer with an empty fee tank pays from their first click.

  4. Invoice documents, hardened links & encrypted backup. Numbers, dates, customers and line items in the private body; revocable links; records restorable on a new device.

  5. SDK + MCP server. Software agents issue and settle invoices the way people do.

Ordering set by an external product audit — the full triage ships in the repo (docs/AUDIT-RESPONSE.md).
